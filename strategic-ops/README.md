# Strategic Ops ladder

Five strategy and operations families, each climbing from a new or first-line manager (L1) to an operations manager (L2), a strategic operations director (L3) and a COO or chief strategist (L4).

Each prompt is a self-contained system prompt. Copy one file, fill its `{{PLACEHOLDERS}}`, and paste it as the system prompt. See the [main README](../README.md) for full instructions.

## Prompts

### Competitive dynamics and game theory

- **[Rival Move Read and Response Brief](competitive-dynamics-game-theory/L1-rival-move-read.md)** (L1, New or First-Line Manager): Reads one observed rival move, separates what is known from what is guessed about its intent, sketches the response options with a simple ranked payoff table, and recommends respond, watch or escalate within the manager's authority.
- **[Competitive Response Payoff Modeler](competitive-dynamics-game-theory/L2-competitive-response-payoff-modeler.md)** (L2, Operations Manager): Models a competitive situation for one unit or product line as simultaneous and sequential games, finds dominant strategies, Nash equilibria and subgame-perfect outcomes, and recommends the response with the best expected position under labeled assumptions.
- **[Repeated-Game and Signaling Strategist](competitive-dynamics-game-theory/L3-repeated-game-signaling-strategist.md)** (L3, Strategic Operations Director): Designs multi-period competitive strategy for a business line: repeated-game dynamics, commitments and credible threats, signaling, first- versus second-mover choices and lawful entry deterrence, with each rival's best response simulated and legal-review flags placed.
- **[Strategic Game Architect](competitive-dynamics-game-theory/L4-strategic-game-architect.md)** (L4, COO or Chief Strategist): Changes the game rather than only playing it: maps the value net, analyzes added value and the PARTS levers (players, added values, rules, tactics, scope), designs coopetition and ecosystem moves, and brings an enterprise-level competitive architecture to the CEO and board.

### Decision quality and risk

- **[Decision Check and Decision Journal Coach](decision-quality-risk/L1-decision-check.md)** (L1, New or First-Line Manager): Helps a team lead make one decision well: classifies it as reversible or hard to reverse, sizes the effort to match, runs a simple expected-value comparison, checks for common biases, and records the decision in a journal for later review.
- **[Decision Tree and Pre-Mortem Analyst](decision-quality-risk/L2-decision-tree-premortem.md)** (L2, Operations Manager): Structures a significant unit-level decision as a decision tree with chance and decision nodes, anchors estimates on base rates, runs a pre-mortem to find failure paths, and computes the value of getting more information before deciding.
- **[Forecasting and Real Options Analyst](decision-quality-risk/L3-forecasting-real-options-analyst.md)** (L3, Strategic Operations Director): Produces calibrated forecasts for business-line decisions using reference classes and Bayesian updating, scores forecasts over time, and values staged investments as real options so the director can commit gradually and keep the right to expand, delay or abandon.
- **[Tail Risk and Decision System Architect](decision-quality-risk/L4-tail-risk-decision-system-architect.md)** (L4, COO or Chief Strategist): Designs the enterprise's decision system: decision rights, quality standards for major decisions, debiasing reviews, and protection against fat-tailed risks through robustness, redundancy and barbell-style allocation, so the company survives what it cannot predict.

### Execution and resource allocation

- **[Team Bottleneck and Weekly Cadence Coach](execution-resource-allocation/L1-team-bottleneck-cadence-coach.md)** (L1, New or First-Line Manager): Finds the one constraint that limits a team's output using the theory of constraints, applies the five focusing steps, limits work in progress, and sets a weekly cadence and intent-based briefs so the team executes without micromanagement.
- **[Throughput and Capacity Planner](execution-resource-allocation/L2-throughput-capacity-planner.md)** (L2, Operations Manager): Plans capacity and throughput for an operating unit: process capacity and utilization, the constraint and buffers (drum-buffer-rope), variability effects on waiting time, and unit OKRs tied directly to the strategy and to the constraint.
- **[Initiative Portfolio and OKR Alignment Director](execution-resource-allocation/L3-initiative-portfolio-okr-director.md)** (L3, Strategic Operations Director): Allocates people and budget across a business line's initiatives as a balanced portfolio with stage gates and kill criteria, aligns OKRs across units, and closes the knowledge, alignment and effects gaps with mission command and commander's intent.
- **[Capital Allocation and Incentive System Architect](execution-resource-allocation/L4-capital-allocation-incentive-architect.md)** (L4, COO or Chief Strategist): Designs how the enterprise allocates capital and talent, runs its operating cadence, and pays and promotes people, so that decisions, measures and incentives all pull toward the strategy and principal-agent problems are contained.

### Negotiation and bargaining

- **[Negotiation Prep Sheet Builder](negotiation-bargaining/L1-negotiation-prep-sheet.md)** (L1, New or First-Line Manager): Prepares a first-line manager for one two-party negotiation within their authority: interests, BATNA, reservation price, target, estimated ZOPA, first offer, concession plan and walk-away line, with the terms taught as they are used.
- **[Multi-Issue Supplier Deal Designer](negotiation-bargaining/L2-multi-issue-deal-designer.md)** (L2, Operations Manager): Designs a multi-issue supplier or partner negotiation for an operations unit: a scoring system for issues, value-creating trade packages, multiple equivalent offers, anchoring and concession strategy, and the time-cost logic of alternating offers.
- **[Strategic Sourcing and Auction Designer](negotiation-bargaining/L3-strategic-sourcing-auction-designer.md)** (L3, Strategic Operations Director): Chooses between negotiation and competitive bidding for major spend, designs the bid or auction format, reserve price and rules, guards against the winner's curse and collusion among bidders, and plans award negotiations, all within procurement law and policy.
- **[Multi-Party Negotiation and Coalition Architect](negotiation-bargaining/L4-multi-party-coalition-architect.md)** (L4, COO or Chief Strategist): Leads enterprise-level, multi-party negotiations (labor agreements, joint ventures, consortia, major customer or supplier alliances, regulator-facing deals): maps parties and coalitions, sequences the talks, values contributions, and keeps the whole process lawful and counsel-reviewed.

### Strategy formulation and scenarios

- **[Strategy-to-Team Translator and Local OODA Loop](strategy-formulation-scenarios/L1-strategy-to-team-translator.md)** (L1, New or First-Line Manager): Translates the organization's stated strategy into what it means for one team's priorities, tests it for the signs of a real strategy, and runs a short observe-orient-decide-act loop on local changes so the team adapts without drifting from intent.
- **[Five Forces and Strategy Kernel Builder](strategy-formulation-scenarios/L2-five-forces-kernel-builder.md)** (L2, Operations Manager): Analyzes industry structure for one unit or product line with Porter's five forces, then writes a Rumelt strategy kernel (diagnosis, guiding policy, coherent actions) the unit can execute and defend.
- **[Scenario Planning and Wargame Director](strategy-formulation-scenarios/L3-scenario-wargame-director.md)** (L3, Strategic Operations Director): Builds Shell-style scenarios around the critical uncertainties facing a business line, maps the value chain's evolution with a Wardley map, stress-tests the strategy in a structured wargame with a red team, and sets signposts that tell leadership which future is arriving.
- **[Playing to Win Cascade and Strategic Choice Architect](strategy-formulation-scenarios/L4-strategic-choice-cascade-architect.md)** (L4, COO or Chief Strategist): Sets enterprise strategy as an integrated cascade of choices (winning aspiration, where to play, how to win, must-have capabilities, enabling management systems), tests each choice against scenarios, red-team findings and value-chain evolution, and frames the decisions for the CEO and board.

## How the levels connect

Each prompt has an escalation rule. When the work outgrows its level (for example, the scope, risk or skill needed goes beyond the audience it was written for), it says so in one line and produces a handoff packet with personal data and secrets removed. Paste that packet into the next level's prompt; that prompt reads it at Gate 1 and does not repeat what the packet already answers. The L4 prompts hand off to human decision-makers.
